How it works

Three steps, from prep to follow‑up

  1. 1

    Load the deal context

    Before the call, fill in a brief: the account, deal stage, goal, CRM notes and emails, your pricing and objection playbook, competitors, and the questions you plan to ask. Paste text or upload .txt/.md files.

  2. 2

    Press Start on your call

    Join your Zoom, Teams or Meet call as usual and press Start. Closewing only listens after you press Start, and it shows a private “assisting” indicator the whole time.

  3. 3

    Get cues live, then a recap

    As the conversation moves, Closewing suggests what to ask next and how to answer questions and objections. When you end the call you get a summary, key points, objections, next steps and a draft follow-up email.

Private by design

Your cues stay on your screen, not in the meeting

Closewing is a private tool for the rep. It doesn’t join as a participant or post anything to the meeting.

  • No bot participant. Closewing never joins the call, posts to chat, or draws on the meeting page.
  • Hidden from screen share on macOS (tested); Windows support in testing. The desktop overlay and main window ask the OS to exclude them from screen capture. This is on by default and tested on macOS. Windows uses the same OS setting but is still being tested, so check your setup before you rely on it.
  • Presenting warnings (extension). If you start presenting in Meet, Teams or Zoom in the browser, Closewing reminds you to share a tab or window instead of your whole screen.
  • You always know when it’s listening. It only starts when you press Start, and it shows an “assisting” indicator in the panel or overlay, on the toolbar badge, and in the tray or menu bar.
  • Your data stays yours. With your own AI keys, audio and text go straight from your device to the providers you choose. With Closewing’s hosted AI (coming with launch), audio still streams directly from your device to the speech-to-text provider, and our server passes suggestion requests to the AI provider without storing or logging them. No audio is stored, and call history stays on your device.

The honest limits

A browser extension can’t hide its side panel if you share your entire screen. Closewing can only warn you, so share a tab or a single window. On desktop, hiding from screen share is tested on macOS; Windows support is still in testing, and some capture tools ignore the OS exclusion flag (some macOS 15+ ScreenCaptureKit setups among them), so test with your meeting app before you rely on it.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

Is it legal to use Closewing on calls? Do I need consent?

It depends on where you and the other participants are. Many places require consent from everyone on a call before it is recorded or transcribed, including several US states (“two-party” or “all-party” consent) and many countries. You are responsible for getting consent where it’s required. Closewing asks you to acknowledge this on first run, and Settings includes an optional consent notice you can paste into the meeting chat.

Closewing doesn’t give legal advice. If you’re unsure, check with your legal team before using it on customer calls.

Can the other people on the call see Closewing?

Closewing never joins as a participant, posts to chat, or adds anything to the meeting page. On desktop, Closewing’s windows are hidden from screen share on macOS (tested). Windows support uses the same OS setting and is still being tested. In Chrome, sharing a tab or window keeps the side panel out of view, but sharing your entire screen will show it. Some capture tools ignore the OS exclusion flag, so test your setup first. Closewing is meant to help you prepare and respond; it isn’t meant to hide the fact that you’re using notes or tools.

Does Closewing record my calls?

Closewing transcribes audio live so it can suggest cues, and it saves the transcript, summary and notes to History on your device. It doesn’t record video. Audio is sent to the speech-to-text provider for transcription: with your own Deepgram key, or with Closewing’s hosted AI once it launches. With hosted AI, audio streams from your device directly to Deepgram, and Closewing’s server only counts minutes; it doesn’t store or log transcripts.

What’s the difference between the extension and the desktop app?

The free Chrome extension works with browser calls in Chrome (Google Meet, Microsoft Teams on the web and Zoom’s web client) and shows cues in Chrome’s side panel. The desktop app for Mac and Windows works with the Zoom and Microsoft Teams desktop apps and shows cues in an always-on-top overlay. Its windows are hidden from screen share on macOS (tested); Windows support is in testing.

Do I need my own AI keys?

No. Hosted AI needs no keys: 60 minutes a month on Free, 600 on Pro Hosted and 1,000 per seat on Team Hosted. Or bring your own Deepgram/OpenAI/Gemini keys for unlimited use on Pro (your own keys), Pro Hosted, Team or Business. Free includes the hosted minutes only. Accounts and hosted AI are built and coming with launch. A demo call mode works with no keys and no account.

How does “Pro (your own keys)” billing work?

You pay Closewing $29/month ($24/month billed yearly) for the apps and features. Speech-to-text and AI usage go on your own Deepgram and OpenAI or Google Gemini accounts: you pay those providers directly, at their prices and under their terms, and Closewing never sees or marks up those charges. Your calls go straight from your device to those providers. As a rough guide, 10 hours of calls is about $9 of Deepgram transcription (both sides of the call) plus around $1–3 of AI at small-model list prices; check the providers’ current pricing.

What happens if I use up my hosted minutes?

Transcription stops for the rest of that call with a clear message; cards from your playbook and the summary still work. Minutes reset on the 1st of each month (UTC). Move up a plan, or use your own keys on Pro (your own keys) and above.

Can I use Closewing on more than one computer, or share it?

Your Closewing account is your license, not the download. Each seat can be signed in on 2 devices (say, your laptop’s Chrome and the desktop app) and run one live call at a time. Sign in on a third device and Closewing asks which one to sign out; Settings lists your devices so you can sign one out remotely. Teams get one seat per rep, and the admin invites people and assigns seats. The demo call works without an account.

How accurate are the suggestions?

Suggestions are only as good as the brief you give Closewing and the quality of the audio. AI output can be wrong, so treat cues as prompts, not scripts, and double-check facts like pricing and security claims before you say them. Headphones help a lot with transcription quality.
